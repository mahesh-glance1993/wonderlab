Curiosity Check: "Why does Violet bend more than Red?"

KEY LAW

Inside empty space, all light travels at exactly 300,000 km/s. But entering glass, light slows down! Shorter wavelengths (Violet) interact stronger with the glass atoms and crawl slower, bending steeply. Longer wavelengths (Red) rush through with fewer bumps, bending gently!

palette White light is a secret crowd!

Sunlight and flashlight rays look plain white, but they actually carry seven vibrant color wavelengths bundled together: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, and Red (VIBGYOR).

directions_run Running into deep water

Imagine running on smooth pavement, then sprinting into knee-deep water. You naturally slow down and your stride pivots! That pivot is refraction.

shape_line The Triangular Shape

Because a prism’s sides aren’t parallel (like window panes), light bends once when entering, and bends again when exiting—spreading the colors out like an unfolded peacock fan!
